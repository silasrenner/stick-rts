# Creative Direction Primer — v0.1

## Project Identity

A bright, side-view fantasy real-time strategy game about two rival civilizations fighting across a beautiful frontier landscape.

The game combines:

- The broad, heroic grasslands and medieval scale of the plains surrounding Rohan
- The clean environmental readability and adventurous spirit of a classic Zelda overworld
- The strong silhouettes, colorful faction identities, and visual clarity of League of Legends
- A small amount of Kirby-like warmth, charm, and playful animation

The world should feel heroic, welcoming, colorful, and alive. It may contain ancient history and serious conflict, but it should not feel bleak, oppressive, or grimdark.

## Core Experience

The player commands a compact army across a primarily horizontal battlefield. Units and structures occupy a narrow playable strip near the bottom of the screen, while a large scenic environment creates a sense of scale and place.

The world should make the player feel that they are controlling a small but important conflict within a much larger fantasy landscape.

The main gameplay elements are:

- Miners
- Warriors
- Archers
- Three distinct heroes
- Mines
- Defensive turrets
- Supply buildings
- A central magical Core

Units must remain easy to identify at small scale. Character silhouettes, equipment, faction colors, and animation should communicate roles before fine details do.

---

## World Premise

The game takes place in a fertile frontier of rolling plains, wooded hills, rivers, ruins, and distant mountains.

This region contains valuable mineral deposits and ancient sources of magical energy. Settlements are built around powerful structures known as Cores, which provide energy, protection, prosperity, and political legitimacy to the surrounding territory.

Two neighboring powers claim responsibility for protecting and governing the frontier. Their cultures are different, but neither faction is inherently evil. Both believe that controlling the land and its Cores is necessary for the safety and future of their people.

The central conflict should feel like a genuine rivalry between two heroic civilizations rather than a conventional battle between good and evil.

The deeper history of the Cores, the frontier, and the ancient ruins remains open for future development.

## Faction One: The Sunmeadow League

### Identity

The Sunmeadow League is a confederation of prosperous plains settlements, farming communities, mounted houses, and civic strongholds.

They see the frontier as a place that should be cultivated, connected, and defended through cooperation. Their culture values optimism, mobility, hospitality, honorable service, and visible civic pride.

They are not naïve or harmless. Their brightly colored armies are organized, ambitious, and determined to expand the League’s influence.

### Visual Language

- Rounded and welcoming shapes
- Green, cream, warm gold, and cobalt-blue accents
- Pale stone, oak timber, polished bronze, and painted cloth
- Sun, horse, wheat, wind, and lantern motifs
- Tall banners, green roofs, open courtyards, and warm lights
- Clean, orderly structures integrated into the plains

Their silhouettes should feel upright, open, mobile, and heroic.

### Units

- **Miner:** A practical worker with a bronze helmet, pickaxe, utility belt, and small lantern. Friendly and industrious rather than dirty or miserable.
- **Warrior:** A disciplined infantry fighter with a round shield and spear or sword. The shield provides the unit’s clearest visual identifier.
- **Archer:** A lightly equipped plains scout with a longbow, short cloak, and clean triangular silhouette.

### Heroes

- **Banner Knight:** A mounted or heavily armored leader who inspires nearby units through banners, commands, and defensive leadership.
- **Wind-Speaker:** A graceful magical hero associated with wind, movement, support, and the natural energy of the open plains.
- **Dawnblade Champion:** A bold frontline hero using a luminous sword, sun imagery, and direct heroic power.

### Buildings

- **Mine:** A pale-stone mine entrance with wooden supports, carts, lanterns, and a small faction banner.
- **Turret / Watchtower:** A rounded stone tower with a green roof, open firing platform, and prominent banner.
- **Supply Building / Hearthhall:** A welcoming timber-and-stone hall with stored grain, crates, barrels, carts, and chimney smoke.
- **Core / Sunspire:** A luminous civic stronghold with layered towers, green roofs, golden details, banners, and a clearly visible magical center.

## Faction Two: The Bramblecrest Wardens

### Identity

The Bramblecrest Wardens are a coalition of wooded hill clans, frontier settlements, stone-border guardians, rangers, and craftspeople.

They see the frontier as something that must be understood, respected, and defended rather than reorganized. Their culture values endurance, local knowledge, adaptability, craftsmanship, ancestry, and responsibility to the land.

They are not savage, corrupt, or villainous. They are proud defenders who believe the Sunmeadow League is expanding too quickly and treating ancient territory as a resource to be claimed.

### Visual Language

- Sturdier and slightly more angular silhouettes
- Pine green, brick red, warm brown, iron gray, and pale stone
- Timber palisades, rugged masonry, leather, iron, and carved wood
- Antler, stag, thorn, rune, pine, and standing-stone motifs
- Layered roofs, fortified halls, watch posts, and practical frontier equipment
- Nature incorporated into structures without becoming an exaggerated forest faction

Their silhouettes should feel grounded, defensive, resourceful, and resilient.

### Units

- **Miner:** A rugged worker with an iron helmet, pickaxe, heavy gloves, and a prominent lantern.
- **Warrior:** A broad defensive fighter with a sturdy shield and axe, spear, or short sword.
- **Archer:** A compact ranger with a hooded cloak, practical bow, and quick, low-profile stance.

### Heroes

- **Stag-Captain:** A commanding frontline leader with an antler-inspired helm, heavy weapon, shield, or ceremonial banner.
- **Runesmith Guardian:** A defensive magical hero who uses carved stones, protective runes, crafted equipment, and grounded elemental power.
- **Scout Champion:** A fast ranger hero built around mobility, observation, ambush, and precise attacks.

### Buildings

- **Iron Mine:** A rugged mine built into rock, supported with timber beams, tracks, carts, lanterns, and smoke.
- **Warden Tower:** A fortified stone-and-timber structure with a compact roof, defensive platform, palisade elements, and a stag banner.
- **Warden Hall:** A low, sturdy communal building surrounded by supplies, carved posts, tools, and protective fencing.
- **Runewood Core:** A stronghold built around glowing standing stones, carved runes, timber architecture, and restrained natural growth.

## First Map: The Open Frontier

The initial map should establish the entire visual identity of the game.

### Environment

A bright grassland frontier beneath a broad blue sky.

The scene includes:

- A flat and readable combat lane in the foreground
- Tall grasses and wildflowers along the edges
- Rolling green hills in the middle distance
- A few large, windswept trees
- A shallow stream or small pond
- Wooden fences, road markers, stones, and occasional shrubs
- One ruined stone watchtower or similar historical landmark
- A distant tree line
- Layered blue mountains
- Large, soft clouds and warm daylight

The landscape should feel inhabited but not crowded. Environmental objects should reinforce depth without interfering with the readability of combat.

### Layering

- **Gameplay layer:** Units, buildings, projectiles, resources, grass edges, and immediate battlefield props.
- **Middle-distance layer:** Trees, fences, streams, ruins, hills, roads, and distant settlements.
- **Background layer:** Forest silhouettes, mountain ranges, clouds, sunlight, and the broad sky.

Subtle parallax movement should reinforce scale.

## Pixel-Art Direction

The target is:

> 8-bit spirit with polished 16-bit execution.

The art should not be limited by strict historical console specifications. The goal is to preserve the clarity, intentional pixel placement, and iconic shapes of older pixel games while allowing enough detail for modern animation and faction differentiation.

### Key Principles

- Use crisp pixel clusters rather than blurred or painterly rendering
- Prioritize silhouettes before internal detail
- Use controlled, bright palettes rather than unlimited colors
- Keep faction colors recognizable during crowded battles
- Maintain strong foreground/background separation
- Avoid visual noise directly behind units
- Allow heroes to be more detailed, but preserve the same scale language
- Buildings should read clearly through overall shape, roofline, and faction emblem

### Character Proportions

Characters should be compact, sturdy, and slightly stylized.

They may have:

- Slightly oversized helmets, shields, bows, tools, and capes
- Readable heads and expressive poses
- Small amounts of squash, bounce, and secondary motion
- Warm, charming personality without becoming infantile or comedic

The Kirby influence should appear through friendliness, motion, color, and expressive timing—not through copying Kirby character anatomy.

---

## Animation Direction

Animation should make the world feel cheerful and responsive.

Examples include:

- Banners moving in the wind
- Grass and flowers gently swaying
- Small birds or distant creatures crossing the landscape
- Miners adjusting helmets or struggling slightly with heavy tools
- Warriors briefly bracing behind shields
- Archers leaning into shots
- Heroes receiving slightly exaggerated anticipation and follow-through
- Supply buildings emitting chimney smoke
- Cores pulsing with calm magical energy

Defeat animations should remain readable without becoming graphic or gruesome.

## Tone Boundaries

### Emphasize

- Adventure
- Heroism
- Rivalry
- Natural beauty
- Warmth
- Exploration
- Civic pride
- Distinct cultures
- Old ruins and unexplained history
- Serious stakes presented through an inviting aesthetic

### Avoid

- Grimdark presentation
- Horror-driven worldbuilding
- Heavy desaturation
- Excessive mud, blood, decay, or misery
- A simplistic good-versus-evil conflict
- Generic faceless medieval armies
- Overly realistic armor and architecture
- Excessive visual detail that compromises gameplay readability
- Direct imitation of existing copyrighted characters, locations, or faction designs

## Agent Operating Instructions

Treat this document and the supplied moodboards as the current creative-direction baseline.

When generating new ideas:

- Preserve the bright, heroic, adventurous tone.
- Maintain clear visual contrast between the two factions.
- Keep both factions sympathetic and culturally coherent.
- Prioritize gameplay readability over ornamental detail.
- Treat the initial grassland frontier as the visual anchor for the world.
- Do not introduce major new factions, technologies, races, or cosmological lore as established canon without explicit approval.
- Clearly label new material as either **Established direction**, **Interpretation**, or **New proposal**.
- When proposing assets, explain how they fit the faction’s shape language, materials, palette, and cultural identity.
- Use the reference images for mood, hierarchy, palette, and visual language—not as exact production-ready sprite sheets.
- Flag any proposal that conflicts with the established tone or reduces unit readability.

## One-Paragraph Agent Summary

This is a bright, pixel-inspired side-view fantasy RTS set across sweeping green frontier plains. Its tone blends heroic medieval adventure, colorful modern gameplay readability, classic overworld charm, and gentle character warmth. The main conflict is between the optimistic, civic-minded Sunmeadow League and the rugged, land-conscious Bramblecrest Wardens. Neither faction is evil; each believes it is the rightful steward of a contested region powered by valuable mines and ancient magical Cores. Visual development should prioritize clear silhouettes, expressive small-scale units, distinct faction palettes, charming animation, readable structures, and a large scenic background of grasslands, ruins, streams, trees, blue mountains, and open sky.

The only part that remains explicitly provisional is the deeper lore behind the Cores and the exact reason the rivalry has escalated into open warfare. Everything else is developed enough to guide early environment, unit, building, animation, and UI work without locking the project into a story that has not been fully explored.
