Executive diagnosis
--------------------
An 8-unit launch is enough to start combat, but not enough to establish durable
local superiority at the defensive line.

The repeated pattern is:

  1. Both sides launch with about eight combat units.
  2. They make contact, normally targeting combat units rather than turrets
     or the core.
  3. Attrition quickly reduces the attacker below five combat units.
  4. Reinforcements exist, but arrive in small, discontinuous packets rather
     than as one battlefield mass.
  5. Attack exits based primarily on global living combat-unit count.
  6. The remaining forward force receives Defend and is redirected to the
     home-side formation.
  7. The defender reforms under turret advantage while the attacker rebuilds
     from four to eight.
  8. The same loop repeats.

This is no longer primarily a miner-production or first-army problem. Current
production creates armies and continued combat purchases. The missing strategic
information is whether the current engagement is producing a favorable,
progressing frontline, rather than whether the team owns five combat units
anywhere on the map.

Structural progress
-------------------
The observed attacks are mostly unit trades, with rare shallow turret damage.

- Early cycles in both seeds show zero turret/core damage.
- Seed 701: AI's fourth cycle dealt 96 turret HP; later cycles produced only
  sporadic small turret hits.
- Seed 702: structural contact was still rarer: one player cycle dealt 32
  turret HP and one late AI cycle dealt 48.
- Both cores remained at 2,000 / 2,000 HP after 3,000 seconds in both
  unresolved diagnostic matches.

Targeting is not prematurely choosing the core. supply.js intentionally
prioritizes the starting turret, then combat units, miners, structures, then
the statue/core. The issue is that local victories rarely leave enough grouped
force to capitalize before the strategic sustain condition ends the push.

Reinforcement behavior
----------------------
Production is occurring, but it is not reliably converted into battlefield mass.

In seed 701's representative AI attack:

- Launch began at 8 combat.
- AI briefly reached 10 total combat units during the attack.
- Active contact repeatedly collapsed to zero before restarting.
- At nine total combat, only one unit was at a turret.
- During turret damage, only two units were striking it.

Total friendly combat therefore does not represent combat mass at the actual
frontline. Attack movement sends units toward enemy home independently; target
acquisition fragments them around contacts. Formation naturally regroups only
when Defend is active. The Attack-to-Defend transition redirects all survivors
home through commands.js and formation.js.

Attack transition analysis
--------------------------
For the representative seed-701 AI cycle, the decisive transition was the
sustain boundary:

  combat units: 4
  sustain threshold: 5
  command: attack -> defend
  goal: attack -> Build Army

Relevant current paths:

- behavior.js: pickCommand() sustains only at combat >= attackSustainCombatUnits
  and otherwise launches only at combat >= attackLaunchCombatUnits.
- goals.js likewise returns Build Army once a committed attack is below sustain.
- commands.js applies the new command to every living unit.
- formation.js changes their objective from enemy-home attack formation to the
  home-side defensive line.

Hysteresis is functioning as configured. The problem is not oscillation at
8/7/6 combat. It is that hysteresis is based on an incomplete signal: global
headcount. Some exits may also be defensive overrides; a seed-702 player attack
exited at five due to such a higher-priority condition. The dominant pattern is
nonetheless post-attrition exit at four combat.

Defender interaction
--------------------
Defender behavior materially creates the stalemate but is not, by itself, a
separate defender-policy defect.

Once the attacker drops below sustain, the attacker resets while the defender:

- already occupies or approaches its turret-side line,
- has a shorter reinforcement route,
- retains turret advantage, and
- faces no continuing pressure from the surviving attacker.

This produces the stable loop:

  local attacker mass declines
  -> global attacker count falls below five
  -> attacker resets
  -> defender retains/reforms home-line position
  -> next eight-unit attack meets a reset defense

Available signals and their limitation
---------------------------------------
Current assessment already provides:

- friendlyPower
- estimatedEnemyPower
- living composition
- current visible and remembered enemy composition
- enemyNearHome
- recovery state
- command and strategic goal

However:

- friendlyPower is global friendly power, not frontline/engaged power;
- estimatedEnemyPower comes from visible/remembered composition, not a current
  local force;
- mobile memory is intentionally short-lived and cannot become false certainty;
- no bounded data records recent confirmed casualties, current engagement
  participation, forward mass, target class, or objective damage.

Global relativeCombatStrength alone is therefore not sufficient. In the seed-701
cycle, AI had 8–10 total combat units while only one or two had reached the
turret.

Recommended missing reusable heuristic
---------------------------------------
Recommend one new reusable assessment primitive: engagementMomentum.

This should be a bounded, team-owned snapshot, not a procedural rule such as
"if attack fails twice". Minimum raw representation:

  recentContactAt
  friendlyCombatLossesInWindow
  confirmedEnemyCombatLossesInWindow
  friendlyCombatCurrentlyEngaged
  visibleEnemyCombatAtEngagement
  forwardMostFriendlyPosition / objective-distance progress
  confirmedObjectiveDamageInWindow

Information boundary:

- Friendly losses are authoritative friendly-state facts.
- Enemy losses must be counted only when confirmed through current team
  engagement/visibility.
- Local enemy force must use current team vision.
- Forward position is friendly state.
- Damage to a visible/attacked turret, structure, or core may be recorded as a
  known combat outcome, not read from hidden world state.

A later heuristic can derive a normalized outcome such as:

  engagementMomentum =
      recent confirmed enemy attrition
    + confirmed objective progress
    - recent friendly attrition
    - lack of engaged friendly mass at the frontline

No weights or behavior should be selected in this diagnostic scope.

Why this is the next smallest general addition
-----------------------------------------------
It measures the actual battlefield outcome, rather than counting previous policy
outcomes. It can later inform:

- Attack: reinforce, hold, regroup, sustain, or abandon based on productive
  current pressure.
- Defend: distinguish stabilizing defense from a collapsing local front.
- Recover: prioritize rebuilding while recent exchange remains negative.
- Future Raven/scouting: improve confidence in local force estimates without
  changing team-limited perception.

It preserves the intended architecture:

  team perception / team-known events
  -> assessment: engagement snapshot
  -> goals and candidate utilities weight it
  -> feasibility remains unchanged
  -> deterministic selection/execution remains unchanged
